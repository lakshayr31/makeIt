# Copy

Xero talks to small business owners and their advisors. The voice is plain, confident and human: it names an outcome the owner cares about (getting paid, less admin, clarity on cashflow) in everyday words.

## Rules

**CP1 MUST: Plain, confident, human voice; sentence case; no exclamation marks, superlatives ("best") or competitor wording.**
- How to apply: short sentences, everyday words, active voice, second person ("you", "your") where natural. One idea per line.
- Avoid: exclamation marks, "best", "#1", "ultimate", "revolutionary", jargon ("leverage", "synergy"), fear-mongering, ALL CAPS shouting, emoji.
- Reviewer check: any exclamation mark or superlative is a failure; copied competitor phrasing is a failure.
- Why: confidence comes from a clear, specific claim, not volume.

**CP2 MUST: Talk about Xero's product benefit for small business; never the competitor's product or category.**
- How to apply: when adapting another ad, keep its rhetorical shape and intent but swap the subject to the equivalent Xero claim (a benefit + a Xero offer, e.g. a pain relieved, a task automated, a free trial). Never mention the competitor's category (music, travel, food...) unless it is the customer's own business type.
- Proof: prefer product proof (UI, a concrete number about the owner's outcome) over reviews and ratings.
- Offers: state them plainly ("90% off for 6 months", "Try Xero for free"); do not invent offers that the product brief does not give.

## Structure
- Headline: the outcome or claim, 2-8 words, sentence case, ends with a full stop in generated ads.
- Support: how it works or why to believe it, one line.
- CTA: verb first, at most 22 chars.
- Keep total words low; feed ads are read in under two seconds.

## Angles for copy variants
- Outcome-led ("Get paid faster."), pain-led ("Stop chasing invoices."), proof-led (a concrete product capability or number).
